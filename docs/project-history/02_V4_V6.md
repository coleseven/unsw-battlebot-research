# 2. v4–v6: coordination, economy and unified search

## Why the research changed direction

The September 24 competitor review examined 71 newer ranked games of submission 3974: **32 wins, 39 losses**, of which 31 losses reached the round limit. The opponent's longest dragon typically beat ours by a small margin, while our team ate and split more but lost much more length. The report estimated about 101 friendly-dragon deaths per game versus 44 for opponents. This motivated coordination and concentration, rather than merely more reproduction.

The review also read public competitor repositories and assembled frozen sparring opponents. Those source-based observations are distinct from replay inferences and from actually running the opponents. An unused `SPLIT_UNTIL = 300` constant in nicked was initially mistaken for live behavior; the record corrected this before interpreting the corresponding experiment. The tested 300 cutoff was therefore our hypothesis, not a faithful reproduction of that opponent.

Sources: [competitor review](snapshot-2026-10-03/COMPETITOR_RESEARCH.md), [strategy alternatives](snapshot-2026-10-03/STRATEGY_RESEARCH.md), [opponent ladder](snapshot-2026-10-03/OPPONENT_LADDER.md).

Sonar initially packed public location, approximate length and a small checksum into a 32-bit message. Beacon-only generated 1,125 pings, including 385 friendly and 110 enemy hits; it preserved the complete action traces of the no-sonar control. Routing was active after the configured late-game threshold. This was a clean feasibility result, not a win-rate result.

The additive package removed the unsuccessful grower schedule while retaining the same local feeding and sonar mechanisms. This distinction matters: “v4 feeding worked” is too broad. The retained evidence supported trying an improvement over live **v2**, whose weaker mover was the main difference, while the required winning comparison against v3 was tied.

## Replay attribution: where king length came from

`bcsim.feed_trace` reconstructed body positions and associated food creation with natural spawns or specific corpses. Six selected top-team round-limit wins showed final kings obtaining **57.6–100%** of their post-400 consumed pearls from friendly corpses. The tool reported no unknown origins in those games.

This established substantial late resource transfer, but not its intent: deliberate feeding, route congestion and tactical deaths can all create friendly corpses. Tracking only the final king's ID also misses length inherited in a split. The later top-five review strengthened the need for lineage-aware accounting.

## v5: many strategic wrappers, no general breakthrough

The first v5 combination joined a Trauma scout with a sparse round-400 king policy. Across 11 maps and both sides it scored **15/22 versus additive v4**, **14/22 versus v3**, and **5/22 versus nicked**. Opponent-by-map results were strongly non-transitive: stronghold could be a win against nicked and a loss against additive.

The required full-suite work followed a series of narrower probes. The compact table below preserves those probes without treating them as release evidence.

| Early v5 idea | Distinct recorded result | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| All-dragon hotspot seeking | 2–6 versus additive | Congestion and kelp deaths; rejected |
| No-split hotspot collector | 3–5 | Grew to nine then died in a one-way corridor; rejected |
| Split-and-export Trauma scout | 2–0 on Trauma versus additive; 1–1 stronghold mirror | Reached fast food and reproduced; useful mechanism, not general validation |
| Sparse early king | 7–5 versus additive over six maps | Lost both stronghold and Trophy sides; mixed |
| Earlier feeding/rendezvous | 6–6 versus early-king control; 9–3 versus additive | No isolated gain over king control; only 12 intended feed actions in direct games |
| Remove sonar/routing | 5–7 versus additive | No evidence to remove sonar wholesale |
| Stricter king-safety package | 5–7 versus both controls | Rejected; election and head filtering were bundled |

| Scout plus king | 2–2 versus additive; 0–2 nicked on Trauma | Nicked games identical to scout alone: king mechanism inactive there |

The Trauma scout collected **451/473 pearls** and split **126/133 times** in its two additive games. It still lost against nicked. Resource access could improve without producing the longest surviving dragon.

## v6: replace layered overrides with a unified score

The proposed v6 core evaluated moves using one bounded search over remembered terrain, food, space, heads, portal paths and the v3 learned score. Split, protection and feeding stayed explicit strategic decisions. The first five-map architecture check improved trajectory margin sharply and won 6/10 against nicked. Its initial 11-map screen won **17/22 versus v5**, **17/22 versus additive**, **12/22 versus nicked**. It failed the no-0/2-map condition and slightly missed a chosen CPU headroom target.

The following search experiments were preserved:

| Change | Recorded outcome before clock correction | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Early expendable scout may try unpaired portal | 17/22 v5, 17/22 additive, 12/22 nicked | Some portal activation, no standalone gain |
| Pocket-exit route bias | 17,18,13 wins respectively | Failed map guards; the scout could escape without subsequently growing |
| Fixed 200-node search, no wall-clock cutoff | CPU limit exceeded in early Big Empty turns | Rejected before strength tests |
| Fixed 96-node search | Same early CPU failure pattern | Rejected before strength tests |
| Post-portal Trauma hotspot pull | 17,17,13 wins | Founder crossed, never split and died at round 54; rejected |
| Unconditional three-step escape check | Peak 85.53M, p99 63.99M | Missed planned headroom; no full strength screen |
| Narrow escape check only at restricted exits | 17,18,11 wins; peak 80.75M, p99 62.40M | Failed strength/map and headroom gates |

These are historical observations under their original harness. They are **not valid proof that the corresponding Python bots could achieve those win rates on the judge**.

### The decisive evaluation correction

The original search budget used host wall time. Under the local fast runtime, more search completed than under the judge's CPU meter. An unrelated-map divergence between almost identical variants exposed this problem. A judge-calibrated deterministic local clock then showed the magnitude:

- Original v6 fell to **2/22 versus v5, 1/22 additive, 0/22 nicked**.
- Narrow escape fell to **1/22, 1/22, 0/22**.
- In the profiled Big Empty sandbox game, the soft cutoff fired on **16,940 of 19,461 moving turns**.

The fix was **fair allocation of the remaining budget across legal moves**, instead of letting the first move consume almost all of it. Fair-search recovered **16/22 versus v5, 17/22 additive, 12/22 nicked v34** under the calibrated clock and won both Default Small sandbox sides. Its held-out results were 16/22 v3, 17/22 ctmes, 7/22 nicked v44. Stronghold and Trauma remained 0–2 against every opponent.

### Split-floor bug and the actual v6 release

The low-population refill branch let even protected kings split at length four whenever population was below 24, regardless of round. A sparse Trauma team never produced a scoring dragon; feeding on stronghold could lower the population and trigger the fed king to split again.

The repair protected late kings and ended refill at round 400. The frozen floor-fix candidate produced:

| Opponent | Floor-fix v6 wins /22 | Previous fair-search wins /22 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Fair-search v6 | 16 | — |
| Additive v4 | 21 | 17 |
| Nicked v34 | 16 | 12 |
| v3 | 21 | 16 |
| ctmes | 21 | 17 |
| Nicked v44 | 8 | 7 |

The 22-game judge suite covered every map and side, with no over-budget turns, invalid-action deaths or guard fallbacks; peak cost was **81.9M points**, memory about 22 MB. This became **versions/v6**, hash `4959b003957277f5`, with a verified ZIP. Intentional empty-action feeding deaths had by then been separated from missing/invalid responses in new sandbox reports.

## v6.1: books, aggression, hubs and exploration

Portal books supplied paired endpoints only after recognizing a seen edge/ID. Portal crossings increased on several maps but did not by themselves solve Trauma. Alone the book scored about evenly with v6, 12/22 direct; a lighter unseen-exit penalty was not better.

Aggression allowed small unprotected dragons to trade into enemy heads and hunt larger targets. Initial even-trade settings greatly increased trades while reducing food and body kills, leaving performance roughly even. Hub books identified maps from observed geometry and directed search toward high-respawn regions. Exploration goals used stale or unseen sectors and increased pull in poor areas.

The combined `v61b-goal-bot` used attack minimum gain one, hunt minimum gain two and hub weight 60. It won **16/22 against v6**, **22/22 additive**, **18/22 nicked v34**, and **8/22 v44**. The root experiment log ended with several checks marked pending. Saved artifacts close those items:

- Held-out comparison: **22/22 versus v3, 20/22 versus ctmes**.
- CPU suite: **22/22 complete, pass=true**, maximum **83,508,055 points**.
- No-goal variant: **15/22 v6, 21/22 additive, 17/22 nicked, 7/22 v44**; resumed after a worker failure.
